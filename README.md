# CS-255_Portfolio
In this project, I was tasked, as a system designer, with designing a system for DriverPass. DriverPass is a driver education business which provides lessons to help students pass their official driver's license test. Their goal was to integrate a cloud-based application into their existing in-person business to give students the ability to manage their lessons, view driver feedback, and access online course materials. They also needed a way for business personnel to manage business processes and receive updates on any changes to official DMV information and requirements. Examining business requirements and diagramming the path that different users interacting with the system would take was intriguing and provided me a deeper level of understanding of the system. Diving into these aspects of the business and its users informed how the integrated system would be structured to include desired functions from the business, along with secure data and payment handling that maintains compliance with legal regulations. The use case diagram I created shows this depth of interaction amongst the different roles in the system with the application itself. This diagram laid the groundwork for the Customer, Administration, and Servicer roles that would be necessary to perform DriverPass' business functions. 

The UML diagrams I created in the System Design Document further provided a solid base for a system design that DriverPass could integrate into its existing business functions. If I were to revisit the diagrams, I would primarily focus on the activity diagrams and further develop the handling of incorrect inputs and data validation. As they are now, they have some events that allow the user to re-enter input if their original input was incorrect; however, with more time I could include more cases of this regarding identity verification and the code sent to the user's email, allowing for multiple attempts before the user being locked out. User needs were considered throughout the entire process of analyzing business processes, needs, and system requirements. When writing the Business Requirements Document, I paid close attention to the functions necessary on the administrative and customer sides. That document informed the UML diagrams for the system, where I was able to explore how the user roles within the system differ and also share functional needs. Being congnizant of these relationships was integral in creating a functional system that meets the requirements of the business along with creating an intuitive platform for students to interact with the business and the services they provide.

When designing software, I've found that analysis is an important piece of every stage of the Systems Development Lifecycle. The initial analysis phase and gathering of requirements provides the foundation for the system architecture. Subsequent phases then dive into deep analysis of users, functions, methods, data, infrastucture, software, and all the relationships and interactions that are necessary for the system to function in accordance with the business needs. The initial iterations of diagrams and documents are constantly iterated on and revised as new elements of the system are revealed through diagramming and analysis of the systems processes. Every deliverable provides an opportunity for further analysis and examination of the system. In future projects, this approach of analyzing, iterating, and revising, is something I want to integrate into every project that I work on. By referencing the examination of project requirements and user needs while iterating through system designs, I hope to create innovative, realistic, and efficient solutions to any project that I face.
